# Video: Combinatorics and Counting Techniques

This video walks through the fundamental counting principles used in combinatorics: the sum rule, product rule, permutations, combinations, the pigeonhole principle, and the binomial theorem, with worked examples from probability and algorithm analysis.

**Key takeaways:**

- Product rule: if task A has m outcomes and task B has n outcomes (independent), there are m×n combined outcomes.
- P(n, r) = n!/(n-r)! for ordered selections; C(n, r) = n!/(r!(n-r)!) for unordered.
- The pigeonhole principle: placing n+1 items into n bins forces at least one bin to have ≥ 2 items — simple but surprisingly powerful in proofs.
- Pascal's triangle as a recursive structure for binomial coefficients: C(n,r) = C(n-1,r-1) + C(n-1,r).
- The binomial theorem: (x + y)^n = Σ C(n,k) x^k y^(n-k).

The video includes a worked example computing the number of 5-card poker hands and using the pigeonhole principle to prove two people in any group of 367 must share a birthday.
